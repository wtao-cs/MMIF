Multi-channel EEG-based depression recognition via micro- and macro-scale  information fusion


Automatic recognition of depression by combining electroencephalography (EEG) with machine learning or deep learning techniques has attracted widespread attention due to its great potential for early diagnosis and timely intervention. However, most existing methods tend to focus on single-scale information and lack the capacity to finely characterize EEG signals at both micro and macro scales. More importantly, the interaction between multiple scales is often overlooked, which constrains models' classification accuracy. To this end, we propose an effective depression recognition approach that seamlessly integrates appropriate micro- and macro-scale characteristics as distinct yet interconnected components within a unified neural network. To form a better representation for multi-channel EEG signals, this framework exploits sample entropy, permutation entropy, and distribution entropy to characterize global complexity and randomness of signal data. Meanwhile, a lightweight, compact convolutional neural network module is devised to learn deep (micro-scale) discriminative representations. The features derived from individual techniques are then deeply integrated through a high-level fusion module to support depression classification. In addition, our method uses a feature-wise aggregation to interactively couple multi-entropy characteristics, thereby enhancing the ability of shallow feature representation. We validate the performance of the proposed methodology on three publicly available datasets. Comprehensive experiments consistently show that our methodology outperforms other advanced competing approaches. The promising results obtained in this research not only highlight the effectiveness of hybrid learning techniques in EEG-based mental health assessment, but also provide important insights for developing more efficient and accessible depression estimation methods.
